The dataset

Release v1: 29,754 claims by 31 public figures, checked against the public record. Every verdict links to the quote and the evidence. Free to use under CC BY 4.0.

In numbers

31public figures

29,754claims extracted from their public output

11,508checkable: a fact, a promise, guidance or a prediction

1,808researched against the public record

1,251resolved, 69% of those researched

50%of verdicts carry a second judge's dissent

A claim is researched when the method went looking for evidence: dated pages from independent sources, found by search and read by a model. It is resolved when that evidence was enough for a verdict. The rest are unresolvable, which means the record was too thin to decide, not that the claim was wrong. The sample was built per subject: up to 60 checkable claims each, chosen to cover the person's main topics and the years on record, so the counts here describe the sample and not everything a person has ever said.

Who said what held up

Each bar is one subject's resolved claims, split by verdict and centred on the mixed ones. Subjects are ordered by their headline score. The figure after each bar is that score, with the number of resolved claims and the confidence band.

Hover or tab to a segment for its count. A verdict of true or mostly true means the claim matched the record the method found; false or mostly false means it did not; mixed means the record supported part of it. Nothing here is a judgement of the person.

The score and its range

A score is a share of resolved claims that held up, weighted by how clear each verdict was, from 0 to 100. The bar through each dot is the 80% interval: with this many claims, the score could plausibly sit anywhere along it. A filled dot is high confidence, a ring is medium.

Where the record runs out

Of every checkable claim extracted, how many were researched, and how many of those the record could settle. Predictions and promises are harder to resolve than factual claims: many are not yet due, and a promise kept quietly leaves little trace.

Posts against pages

1,146 of the checkable claims came from the subject's posts on X, read through the X API; the rest from interviews, talks, articles, filings and transcripts on the open web. Posts were resolved less often: a post is short, undated context is common, and the record it points at is thinner.

What the numbers do not say

A second judge agreeing is consistency, not accuracy. Every verdict was reached by one model judge and read again by a second one; where the second disagreed, the claim carries a flag, and 50% do. The method's agreement with published outcomes is measured separately on the test results page, and it is not a general measure of accuracy.

Unresolvable is not false. 557 researched claims ended without a verdict because the record was too thin. They are left out of every score.

Party-political claims are left out of scores. 525 claims about who will win an election, what a party will do or whether a policy should pass are tagged political in the data and never counted.

Public output only. Claims come from what each person said in public in their professional capacity. Nothing about private life, and no inferred motives. Each subject can respond through the reply route; replies are attached to the report.

Verdicts are made automatically by the published method, versions 0.1.0+4bff4432, 0.1.0+ecd0c1d5, and are not individually reviewed by a person. A verdict is a statement about the record the method found on the day, with links to it, so anyone can check it.

Get the data

Release v1 is three tables, CSV and JSON Lines, under CC BY 4.0: one row per subject, one per score, one per claim with its quote, source URL, verdict, confidence, the judges' flag and the evidence count. Download it from GitHub or Hugging Face. The code and prompts that made it are not published; the method is.

One subject, no key, from the command line or from a model's tool call:

curl "https://epistacklabs.com/v1/lookup?name=Jensen%20Huang"

The developers page describes the lookup, the request queue for names not yet in the database, and the full API.
