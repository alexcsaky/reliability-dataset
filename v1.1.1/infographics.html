Where the record runs out

About 70 in 100 researched claims could be settled.

Up to 60 checkable claims per person were chosen to cover their main topics and years on record, then researched. The rest of the researched claims are unresolvable: the record was too thin to decide, which is not the same as false.

Posts against pages

Posts on X against pages.

873 checkable claims came from posts on X, read through the X API; the rest from interviews, talks, articles, filings and transcripts on the open web. Of the claims that could be settled, 86% of posts and 80% of pages held up. 31% of researched posts and 29% of researched pages were unresolvable.

Read with care

What the numbers do not say.

A second judge agreeing is consistency, not accuracy. Every verdict was reached by one model judge and read again by a second one; where the second disagreed, the claim carries a flag, and 47% do. The method's agreement with published outcomes is measured separately on the test results page, and it is not a general measure of accuracy.

Unresolvable is not false. 1,131 researched claims ended without a verdict because the record was too thin. They are left out of every score.

Party-political claims are scored in reports made from 29 October 2026. 530 claims are tagged political in the data; 1 of them, in reports made before that date, were left out of scores, as the method then required.

Public output only. Claims come from what each person said in public in their professional capacity. Nothing about private life, and no inferred motives. Each person can respond through the reply route; replies are attached to the report.

Verdicts are made automatically by the published method, versions 0.1.0+03d23c0e, 0.1.0+3e03e2df, 0.1.0+3f84d14d, 0.1.0+5c636cf5, 0.1.0+5e91aed6, 0.1.0+7831dd9d, 0.1.0+8bea457b, 0.1.0+c876030c, and are not individually reviewed by a person. A verdict is a statement about the record the method found on the day, with links to it, so anyone can check it.

Open data

Get the data.

Release v1.1.1 is three tables, CSV and JSON Lines, under CC BY 4.0: one row per person, one per score, one per claim with its quote, source URL, verdict, confidence, the judges' flag and the evidence count.

Hugging FaceGitHub

Read every claim in your browser at https://epistacklabs.com/explore; no key, no sign-up. Search the quotes, filter by person, kind of claim and verdict, and open any claim for its evidence.

The code and prompts that made it are not published; the method is. One person, no key, from the command line or from a model's tool call:

curl "https://epistacklabs.com/v1/lookup?name=Jensen%20Huang"

The developers page describes the lookup, the request queue for names not yet in the database, and the full API.
